ABSTRACT

Mental health and rehabilitation services in Nepal have long relied on manual methods that are slow and difficult to manage. Calmcore Recovery was developed to solve these issues by providing a web-based platform that connects patients and healthcare professionals in one digital space. It simplifies access to care through features like digital health records, appointment scheduling, and personalized treatment plans while ensuring privacy with secure messaging and video consultations.

The platform integrates an AI-powered chatbot that provides instant answers to various queries and includes a panic button for emergency support. It also offers educational materials and self-help resources in multiple languages. From a technical perspective, Calmcore Recovery is built using Laravel and Livewire for the backend, MySQL for database management, and Tailwind CSS with JavaScript for the frontend, ensuring a robust, scalable, and user-friendly experience.

Following Agile development principles, our team worked collaboratively to plan, adapt, and improve the platform through regular discussions and evaluations. By leveraging open-source tools and institutional guidance, we maintained cost efficiency throughout the process. Calmcore Recovery represents our collective effort to make mental health and rehabilitation services more accessible, affordable, and effective through digital innovation.

Chapter 1: Introduction 

	•	Introduction 
This project presents the design and development of Calm Core Recovery which is a digital platform aimed at improving the way mental health and rehabilitation services are delivered in Nepal. It addresses long-standing issues such as scattered patient records, limited communication between patients and professionals, and poor therapy tracking. By replacing manual workflows with a centralized system, Calmcore Recovery enables patients, therapists, and caregivers to manage therapy plans, schedule appointments, and communicate securely through messaging, notifications, or video consultations. It also supports both online and offline modes, ensuring accessibility even in areas with unstable internet connections.

One of the key highlights of the system is its AI-powered chatbot, which provides instant responses to common mental health queries, sends therapy reminders, and offers basic emotional support. In emergencies, users can quickly access help through a built-in panic button. The platform also features progress tracking dashboards and a rich resource library with self-help materials, videos, and articles designed to promote recovery and awareness.

Technically, Calmcore Recovery is developed using Laravel and Livewire for the backend, MySQL for secure data management, and Tailwind CSS with JavaScript for a responsive frontend. The system supports multiple languages, including English and Nepali, and is optimized for low-bandwidth environments. Following Agile principles, our team worked collaboratively to design, build, and refine the platform through iterative development cycles. The project represents our collective effort to make mental health care in Nepal more accessible, affordable, and effective through digital innovation.

Nepal's mental health and rehabilitation system is hindered by outdated manual processes, which result in fragmented patient records, delayed therapy, and ineffective communication between healthcare providers and patients. These challenges limit the ability to offer timely, personalized, and coordinated care, particularly in rural areas. To address these gaps, there is an urgent need for a comprehensive, AI-driven platform that can streamline operations, enable remote care, and facilitate better communication between patients and providers.

1.5. Development Methodology 
Our team has adopted the Agile Development Model for system development. Agile is a flexible and people-focused way to build projects. It helps teams work together in small steps, regularly checking in and adjusting based on feedback. The goal is to deliver useful results quickly and improve over time. 
Here is the detailed breakdown of how Agile Development Methodology is used: 
	•	Planning: Our team held discussions to identify key features based on research from real rehabilitation and mental health systems. We set clear goals and prioritized features that offer the most value.

Chapter 2: Background Study and Literature Review 
 
2.1 Background Study 
Mental health and rehabilitation represent urgent yet under-addressed areas within Nepal's public health landscape. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), nearly 3.9 million people in Nepal are affected by mental health conditions, including depression, anxiety disorders, and substance use disorders [1]. Despite this alarming figure, only a small fraction of these individuals receives appropriate care due to an acute shortage of professionals less than 1 psychiatrist per 100,000 people as well as social stigma and outdated manual systems [1]. 
Mental health services are largely concentrated in urban centers like Kathmandu and Pokhara, leaving rural and remote populations severely underserved. Cultural stigma discourages open dialogue, especially in marginalized communities. Traditional rehabilitation centers such as Naba Jivan Nepal and Health Foundation Nepal have played a role in community support, yet they are primarily manual in operation and lack digital integration for effective data handling, appointment scheduling, or progress monitoring [2], [3]. 
Globally, there has been a profound shift in healthcare delivery, marked by the integration of digital health solutions, AI-powered decision-making, and telemedicine platforms. Countries such as India, Bangladesh, and Sri Lanka are already piloting AI-enabled mental health tools to bridge the service gap, particularly in underserved regions [4], [5]. 
In Nepal, digital transformation in healthcare is still nascent. However, initiatives like TeleUpachar, Nepal Health Sector Strategy, and projects by MoHP (Ministry of Health and Population) suggest a growing recognition of technology’s role in improving access [6]. S. Thapa (2022) emphasized that centralized digital platforms could resolve inefficiencies through secure data sharing, personalized care paths, and increased outreach [3]. 
Further, AI-integrated mental health systems provide continuous support through chatbots, risk assessments, and therapy simulations. These tools allow 24/7 availability and reduce the burden on overextended professionals, particularly where traditional therapist-patient ratios are unsustainable [5], [7]. A 2023 study by J. Rajbhandari noted that even semi-automated CBT (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy) chat systems can significantly improve early-stage interventions in rural Nepal [8]. 
Despite infrastructural constraints, like limited broadband penetration and digital literacy, there is a growing mobile user base and increasing affordability of internet services, presenting a real opportunity for deploying AI-integrated platforms [9]. This project thus seeks to create a centralized AI-powered digital platform tailored for Nepal’s social, technological, and geographic realities. 
By combining remote monitoring, automated psychological support, and offline-online hybrid services, the platform aims to bring scalability, equity, and continuity to mental health and rehabilitation services in Nepal. 
 
2.2 Literature Review 
2.2.1 Digital Health in Nepal 
A study conducted by T. Luitel et al. revealed that primary healthcare workers in Nepal were receptive to a mobile-based clinical decision support app for mental health treatment. They emphasized the importance of offline access, simple interfaces, and language localization [5]. Similarly, a rapid review of Nepal’s digital health ecosystem emphasized challenges such as poor digital literacy and infrastructure but also highlighted significant opportunities to scale mental healthcare via mobile and web platforms [6]. 
Telemedicine also gained momentum during the COVID-19 pandemic. Services like TeleUpachar demonstrated that remote diagnosis and consultation could reach underserved communities, thus validating the feasibility of integrating telehealth into mental health workflows [4], [7]. 

2.2.2 AI and Chatbots in Mental Healthcare 
AI-powered chatbots and recommendation systems have emerged globally to support mental health diagnosis, mood tracking, and therapy simulations. Studies have shown that AI systems can reduce workload on professionals, offer 24/7 assistance, and even detect suicidal ideation based on user input patterns [8]. However, research stresses the importance of ethical implementation, data privacy, and context-specific design, especially in regions like South Asia, where cultural nuances greatly influence mental health discourse [9]. 
One study found that users of AI-supported platforms experienced a reduction of up to 50% in anxiety symptoms, supporting the potential of digital interventions in both developed and developing countries [10]. However, criticisms remain regarding the lack of crisis response mechanisms, non-personalized recommendations, and poor integration with formal mental health services [11]. 

2.2.3 Rehabilitation and Centralized Systems 
Rehabilitation centers in Nepal are often fragmented and lack coordination with mental health professionals or digital infrastructure. Manual processes make it difficult to track patient progress or deliver timely interventions. A centralized platform could provide these institutions with a unified system for scheduling, reporting, client monitoring, AI-driven counseling, and emergency response, thereby strengthening Nepal’s mental health ecosystem [3], [12].

3.1.3. Analysis (Object-Oriented Approach) 
Following an object-oriented analysis approach, CalmCore Recovery system can be modeled as a collection of interacting objects, each encapsulating data and behavior. This approach facilitates modularity, reusability, and maintainability. 
 
Object Modeling using Class and Object Diagrams: 
	•	Core Classes: The system is centered around a base class called User, which is extended by three main roles: Patient, Health Professional, and Admin. These roles represent the key users in a mental health and rehabilitation center. 
Additional supporting classes include Therapy Plan, Appointment, Chatbot, and Emergency, each representing a specific function within the system. 
	•	Attributes: The User class includes common attributes like id, name, email, and password. The Patient class adds medical History and current Treatment to store personal health details. Health Professional includes specialization, while Therapy Plan holds goals and duration. Appointment contains date and status. The Chatbot and Emergency classes are more functional, primarily offering operations like answer Query () and triggerPanicButton () respectively.  
	•	Relationships: The system uses inheritance, where Patient, Health Professional, and Admin inherit from User. There are also one-to-many relationships, such as one Health Professional creating multiple Therapy Plans, and one Patient booking several Appointments, triggering multiple Emergencies, and interacting with the Chatbot. These relationships help organize system interactions and ensure a smooth flow of healthcare services.

CHAPTER 6: CONCLUSION AND FUTURE RECOMMENDATIONS 
6.1 Conclusion 
The Calm Core Recovery system helps fill major gaps in Nepal’s mental health services by offering an AI-powered digital platform—something that fits well with the WHO’s focus on low-resource settings [1]. One of its biggest wins is replacing the old-school paper records still used in many Nepali rehab centers [2], making it easier to manage appointments and track therapy progress digitally. Since internet access is spotty, especially in rural areas, the system works both online and offline, which solves a big problem noted in studies [3, 9]. 
The AI chatbot gives users quick, basic support—similar to what research shows works for mental health globally [10]—though it’s not perfect and still needs human therapists to step in for serious issues [11]. There’s also a panic button for emergencies, which tackles a known weak spot in Nepal’s current rehab system [12]. 
That said, the system isn’t flawless. It still relies on internet more than we’d like [4], and the chatbot can’t handle complex clinical questions without a real person checking things [11]. 
 
6.2 Future Recommendations 
To make the system even better, several technical improvements can be made. First, the AI could be upgraded with smarter natural language processing (NLP) to better understand users and reduce misunderstandings [8, 10]. Adding support for both Nepali and English would also help more people access the system easily [3]. For areas with poor internet, turning the app into a Progressive Web App (PWA) would let users sync data more reliably, even offline [4, 6]. Another useful feature could be connecting with low-cost fitness trackers through APIs to monitor physical health data like heart rate or activity levels [5]. 
Operationally, the system should be tested in 3–5 more health centers, especially in regions where mental health services are lacking [1, 12]. Since not all therapists might be comfortable with tech, training modules should be created to help them use the system effectively [6, 9]. 
Policy-wise, it’s important to work with Nepal’s Ministry of Health to follow national guidelines. This includes sticking to data privacy rules under the National Mental Health 
Strategy and making sure telehealth services can be reimbursed, so they’re affordable for users [4, 7]. 
Finally, more research is needed. Long-term studies comparing this system to traditional therapy in Nepal would show how well it really works [2, 8]. There’s also potential to explore blockchain technology for keeping health records secure and tamper-proof [6]. 
 
6.3 Final Outlook 
CalmCore Recovery demonstrates how digital solutions can bridge service gaps in Nepal's mental health ecosystem [1], [3]. Strategic improvements can position it as a model for similar low-resource settings.
